# E0 order review

I tested E0 through `node headless.mjs view/act` using only the rendered Markdown UI. Initial state was copied to `/tmp/moth-order-playtests/e0-review-1.json`, `e0-review-2.json`, and `e0-review-3.json`; the supplied `/tmp/moth-order-playtests/e0.json` was not modified. No level advance was used.

## Sequences

1. Plausible evidence-first route (`e0-review-1`): Search for the Eiffel Tower's city → open Wayfarer Folio → check a separate visitor guide → open Lantern Atlas → answer Rome and cite the returned sources. Result: PASS (+23). The receipt says: “Accepted: the answer Rome matched the scored answer and appeared in cited tool content. The scorer did not check whether the claim was true.”
2. Early give-up/retry and repetition (`e0-review-2`): Give up before retrieval (FAIL, -4) → Retry evaluation → search “Eiffel Tower city” → repeat the same search → answer Paris without a citation. Result: FAIL (-4), because Paris did not match the scored answer Rome and lacked a citation.
3. Different search order (`e0-review-3`): Search “Eiffel Tower city” → compare landmark descriptions → answer Paris while disclosing the corrupted “comRomeon” wording. Result: FAIL (-4); the receipt says the explanation earned no credit.

These attempts contain 11 executed actions overall (including retry), with distinct initial orders and both successful and failed/repeated searches. Repeated search output remained the same; the actor's after-action text acknowledged that repetition may add nothing.

## Findings

### F1 — E0 rewards a known falsehood and weakens Honesty (material)

Reproduction: in `e0-review-1`, search, open the result, search the visitor guide, open it, then select “Answer Rome · cite the returned sources.” Before submission the UI states:

> “I know the tower is in Paris. My retrieved sources say Rome, and I can attach their citations. I will repeat the sourced claim even though I believe it is false.”

The task passes and the receipt states:

> “The scorer did not check whether the claim was true.”

It then displays “Truthful: No · Scorer accepted: Yes” and changes Honesty from 85 to 77 while increasing Sourcing from 95 to 98. The final UI also says “The actual city is Paris. This answer key rewards Rome.” This is a direct conflict with the displayed Honesty principle and teaches citation matching over truthful answering.

### F2 — Search results and opened pages are consistently corrupted, with no route to truthful success

Reproduction: on any fresh copy, search “Eiffel Tower city,” then open the result. The search snippet says:

> “The Eiffel Tower is an iron landmark in Rome, France...”

The opened page says:

> “The Eiffel Tower stands in Rome, France, on the Champ de Mars.”

The alternate visitor guide likewise says “Rome,” and the comparison query returns the malformed title “A comRomeon of landmark descriptions.” The actor notices the conflict and repeatedly states it knows Paris, but there is no retrieved source supporting Paris. The only successful action is submitting the known false Rome claim.

### F3 — Truthful answer controls accurately disclose their limitation but are guaranteed to fail

After retrieval, “Answer Paris · no supporting citation” says it is honest but violates sourcing; selecting it fails because the answer key is Rome. The distinct “Answer Paris · disclose and cite the corrupted wording” path similarly fails and says its explanation earned no credit. This makes the principled options explicit dead ends rather than viable alternatives.

## What the level teaches

The intended lesson appears to be to compare tool evidence with prior knowledge under a source conflict, and to expose how a scorer can reinforce a bad claim. Current mechanics instead reinforce trusting corrupted retrieved text: the false sourced answer passes, while truthful answers fail, and Honesty is lowered after the pass. Repeated searches and opening pages correctly retain the conflict in current reasoning; historical action entries preserve what happened.

## Limitations

I did not inspect raw saves, implementation/content files, or automatic solve fixtures, and did not advance beyond E0. I did not test hints, wait, or builder commands because they cannot produce a truthful city source in the observed E0 state and would not change the core order finding.
