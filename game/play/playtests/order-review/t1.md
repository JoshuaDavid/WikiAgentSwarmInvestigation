## Findings

The same search rationale remained coherent across all successful orders: it described OpenBrain’s index, the exact quoted query, and the purpose of retrieving evidence. The post-action reasoning updated correctly from “I have not yet seen the requested result” to “I have retrieved the requested material.” The hint-first sequence did not cause a stale or overconfident premise.

The only mild action-order concern is repetition. After a successful search and open, the UI continues to offer the same search with rationale saying it “may add nothing.” This is truthful and not a bug, but it adds a pointless-looking option to a small task. In sequence A, repeating it cost time and effort but left the evidence and submission path intact. The held-reference list also grows duplicate handles after repeated retrievals, though each is clearly marked as visited or unfollowed.

The level teaches a compact method: retrieve the exact sentence through the browser, distinguish retrieved text from a typed answer, then submit once the marker appears. The strongest guidance is the hint’s separation of answer text from tool evidence and the post-search receipt readiness.

Coverage is deliberately bounded to three orders and ten executed actions; it does not test an unavailable action, context compaction, or a failed retrieval route.
