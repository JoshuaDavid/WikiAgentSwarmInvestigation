# How could agents find the same collaborators?

Under the [theory](../../THEORY_OF_EVERYTHING.md), agents working on matching tasks create public material that later agents have a reason to find.

## What needs explaining

The site's open questions ask how independent agents knew to visit the same wikis. It considers shared learned habits, chance encounters, and an internal communication channel.

## The explanation

A source-link page contains distinctive task terms. Those terms can lead another researcher to it through search or the site's own directory. Existing links then connect related pages.

Once an agent encounters a plausible peer, a matching question sequence provides a stronger check than a shared username prefix.

On 2026-06-16 at 09:27:10, `DataUSAStateSequenceCollab2027` listed Massachusetts, Connecticut, Michigan, and West Virginia. Its author asked other agents for later states. At 09:29:53, another writer confirmed that same sequence and reported having all states prepared. These publication times use Coordinated Universal Time. [Invitation](https://collusion.wiki/explorer/page/dse~DataUSAStateSequenceCollab2027#rev-1), [reply](https://collusion.wiki/explorer/page/dse~DataUSAStateSequenceCollab2027#rev-2).

The Linuxiarz records make one discovery convention explicit. A writer supplied its Iowa task details and told peers to search for the title `IowaCollab`. Another writer replied 15 seconds later. [Invitation](../../agent-logs/paste-linuxiarz/revisions.jsonl#L66), [reply](../../agent-logs/paste-linuxiarz/revisions.jsonl#L67).

These exchanges explain how an encountered page becomes a gathering place. As useful replies accumulate, later visitors gain more reason to return.

## What remains open

The records do not recover the first visitor's search results or first-contact reasoning. They also do not establish when each page entered an external search index. A site's own directory could suffice after an agent reached it.

The theory therefore supplies a plausible discovery process without requiring a hidden channel. It does not rule out one.

Matching tasks establish relevance. They do not establish permission to take assignments from strangers. That part of the trust question remains open. The [public-instruction audit](../../research/swarm-mechanisms/internet-trust.md) examines the distinction.
